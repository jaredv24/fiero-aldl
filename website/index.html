See what your engine computer sees.

Fiero ALDL turns the diagnostic plug under the dash into a diagnostic bench on your Mac: live gauges, trouble codes in plain English, guided tests, a leak hunt, a stall recorder that catches the moment the engine dies, and a checkup that tells you where to look. Built for the 1985–88 Fiero V6, and works with any 1996-or-newer car through an OBD-II adapter.

      What it does

      A whole diagnostic bench, not just a gauge cluster

      Live dashboard and chart

      RPM, coolant, throttle, MAP, O2, idle air, fuel trims, spark, EGR and battery, decoded with GM's formulas, with a 60-second chart and normal ranges on every gauge.

      Vacuum from the MAP sensor

      A vacuum gauge without the gauge. The checkup reads the idle pattern the way a mechanic reads the needle: steady, low, drifting with the hunt, or flickering.

      Stall recorder

      Always on. When the engine dies, the last 30 seconds are saved and labeled: ignition signal lost, ran lean and died, power dropout, or a slow sag.

      Leak hunt

      Spray one spot at a time and press a button. The app watches the O2 sensor, fuel trim and idle after each spray and ranks the spots that reacted.

      Guided tests

      Step-by-step tests you can run in the driveway: key-on sensor checks, throttle sweep, warm-up, idle stability, A/C and steering load, a 2000 rpm hold, a road test.

      Trouble codes, named

      Fiero codes by name the moment the ECM sets them. On OBD-II cars: stored, pending and permanent codes, freeze frame, readiness monitors and VIN.

      Checks, history and before/after

      Log fuel pressure, timing, compression and repairs, see every test and check over time, and compare a log from before a fix with one from after.

      Any car, with its own normals

      Works with 1996-and-newer cars through an ELM327-style adapter such as the OBDLink SX. Vehicle profiles hold each car's normal values, with sources; Claude can research one for your car.

      GM PID explorer

      Finds the extra readings GM computers answer beyond generic OBD-II and works out what they are by watching them while you rev the engine or switch on the A/C. Read-only.

      Session report

      One page with charts, findings, codes, tests, leak hunt and stall captures, to print or send to someone who knows these cars.

      Ask Claude

      Optional. Claude reads your live data, codes, stall captures, checks and history itself, can start a guided test for you, and explains what to check first. It can't change anything on the car.

      Ask Claude

      A mechanic's eye on your actual numbers

      Ask a question and Claude looks things up in the app on its own: the live readings, trouble codes, stall captures, the checks you've recorded and the car's history. It can start a guided test and tell you what to do at the car. It ranks the likely causes, quotes the numbers behind each one, and puts the cheap checks first.

      Uses your own Anthropic API key, stored in the Mac Keychain. A question usually costs a few cents. Nothing is sent until you click.

      Example answer
      “What do you see, and what should I check?”

      What the data shows

      • Idle swings between about 650 and 1150 rpm on a 6-second cycle.
      • The idle air valve opens to 95 steps as RPM falls, then snaps shut when it recovers. The ECM is chasing the idle, not holding it.
      • Long-term fuel trim climbs to 138, so the ECM is adding fuel: it sees a lean mixture.

      Check first

      1. Vacuum leaks: listen for hissing around the plenum and PCV hoses with the engine idling.
      2. EGR valve: engine off and cool, push the diaphragm up. It should move freely and spring shut.
      3. Throttle body and idle air passage: clean them, and don't touch the factory idle stop screw.
      Getting started

      From download to live data

      Try the demo

      Download the app, open it, pick Demo: Fiero or Demo: Grand Prix and click Connect. Every screen works with no hardware.

      Plug in a cable

      Get a GM 12-pin ALDL to USB cable that reads 160 baud on pin E. Turn its 10k switch on, plug it in under the dash, key on.

      Check the cable

      Choose USB ALDL cable and click Check cable. It finds the right settings and confirms the Fiero's 25-byte frames.

      Newer car?

      Plug an OBD-II adapter such as the OBDLink SX into the port under the dash, choose OBD-II adapter and pick your car under Vehicles.

      Early release. The Fiero readings follow GM's data stream specification for this engine, and the OBD-II side follows the SAE standard. Both are tested against simulated cars. They haven't been checked on a real car yet.

      First open on a Mac: the app isn't signed by Apple, so macOS blocks it. Drag it to Applications, then run this once in Terminal: xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/Fiero ALDL.app"
